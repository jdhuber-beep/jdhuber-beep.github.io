The Explorer covers single planets and moons. This page covers the solar system as a whole: what sits at the center, where the belts are, and the units used to describe distances in space.

The Sun

  • Diameter About 1.39 million km
  • Share of the solar system's mass About 99.8%
  • Surface temperature About 5,500°C
  • Age About 4.6 billion years

Regions of the solar system

Main regions, listed from the Sun outward
Region Where it is What is there
Inner solar system From the Sun out to Mars The four rocky planets
Asteroid belt Between Mars and Jupiter Millions of rocky asteroids and the dwarf planet Ceres
Outer solar system From Jupiter out to Neptune The gas giants and ice giants
Kuiper Belt Past Neptune, about 30 to 50 AU from the Sun Icy bodies, including Pluto and Makemake

Measuring distance in space

  • Astronomical unit (AU) The average distance from Earth to the Sun, about 149.6 million km
  • Speed of light About 300,000 km per second
  • Sunlight to Earth About 8 minutes and 20 seconds
  • Light-year The distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion km
